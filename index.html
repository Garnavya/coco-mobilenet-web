The Capacity Bottleneck

Monolithic vs. Modular Multi-Task Learning on Edge Hardware (8GB VRAM)

Abstract & Core Hypothesis

Modern artificial intelligence relies on massive parameter counts, but consumer-grade edge devices (smartphones, IoT) are severely hardware-constrained. To deploy multi-modal AI on the edge, developers typically choose between forcing multiple tasks through a single lightweight backbone (Monolithic) or sequentially loading task-specific micro-models (Modular).

Hypothesis: Forcing radically different computational tasks—dense spatial geometry (bounding boxes and keypoints) and autoregressive natural language processing—through a strictly constrained 5.4-million parameter network creates a mathematical capacity bottleneck, heavily favoring decoupled micro-model pipelines.

Methodology: The Edge AI Stress Test

Hardware Ceilings

  • GPU Constraint: Execution bounded by an RTX 5050 (8GB VRAM limit).
  • Batch & Resolution: VRAM ceilings forced the Monolithic model (Track A) to standardize on a 256x256 global resolution at a batch size of 64 to prevent CUDA Out-Of-Memory errors.
  • Backbone limit: MobileNetV3 (5.4 million parameters) acts as the unified feature extractor.

Dataset Starvation

  • Modular Advantage: Track B trained independently on full domain-specific COCO datasets (118k+ detection images, 591k+ captions).
  • Monolithic Penalty: Track A required strict triple-annotation overlap (images containing boxes AND keypoints AND captions simultaneously).
  • The Bottleneck: The monolithic dataset was mathematically restricted to just 56,599 usable multi-task images.

Combating Gradient Domination

In monolithic architectures, computing disparate tasks simultaneously introduces severe gradient conflicts. Image captioning relies on Cross-Entropy (CE) loss across a 10,000+ word dictionary, naturally producing high scalar values. Conversely, object detection and pose estimation rely on Mean Squared Error (MSE) for spatial coordinates, outputting tiny fractional percentages.

The Weighting Formula

Without manual intervention, the massive NLP gradients completely drown out the spatial geometry math, causing the backbone to become "blind" to physical structures. A static multiplier was applied to mathematically inflate the spatial constraints:

Ltotal = (50 × Lbox) + (50 × Lpose) + (1 × Lcap)

Epoch Convergence & The Bottleneck Limit

Training was deliberately halted at Epoch 20 to prevent catastrophic language overfitting while observing the physical limits of the shared architecture. The convergence logs expose the exact moment the 5.4 million parameters reached their cognitive capacity.

Box: Geometric Plateau

0.0028

By Epoch 18, bounding box MSE flatlined. The backbone dedicated enough parameters to find the overall subject but refused to refine further, successfully resolving basic spatial geometry.

Pose: The Bottleneck

0.0231

The smoking gun. Pose MSE stalled in the 0.02 range, failing to hit the 0.01 target. The network lacked the residual capacity to resolve 17 complex human joints across a 256x256 global feature map.

Caption: Overfit Threshold

0.8340

Cross-Entropy loss dropped rapidly. Halting at 0.8340 preserved semantic generalization; continuing would have forced the model to memorize the dataset verbatim, destroying real-world inference capability.

Visual Evidence: The Bottleneck in Action

The statistical collapse observed in the metrics translates directly to real-world inference failures. While the Monolithic architecture successfully maps global bounding boxes, it mathematically regresses the complex skeletal keypoints toward the center of the bounding box mass, entirely failing to track articulated limbs.
